# Product brief: confidence to choose

## Product decision

Should Ravelry test clearer support for judging suitability within existing pattern discovery? The potential maker problem is uncertainty about skill requirements, material fit and how much confidence to place in feedback. This remains an unvalidated user-problem hypothesis; public catalogue counts cannot show why someone hesitates.

Makers benefit from a choice they can assess without extra effort. Designers benefit when suitable work reaches interested makers, including beyond established patterns. Popularity can provide reassurance but can also reinforce attention concentration. Redistributing attention alone does not prove a better maker experience.

## Existing functionality and proposed improvement

[Official filter help](https://www.ravelry.com/help/search?query=filters) describes advanced filtering, comparison and saved searches. [Official stash help](https://www.ravelry.com/help/search?query=stash) describes stash-related suggestions. These are publicly accessible official help summaries, not a current authenticated UI audit. Before a build, review the exact current journey and whether existing features already solve the problem.

The refined proposal is an explainable suitability summary in existing results or comparison, driven by explicit preferences. It would show what matches, what is unknown and what supports crowd feedback. Do not label a pattern objectively achievable, treat crowd difficulty as an individual's skill, or penalise a designer for being new. Personal stash ingestion is outside the initial study.

## Why this scope fits the verified data

Read-only pattern search and bulk detail return craft, category, material attributes, dates, recorded activity totals and rating/difficulty averages/counts. The implemented analysis asks four descriptive questions: concentration of recorded activity; age/craft context; availability of evidence counts; and feasibility of opportunity review. Exact results are in the private local findings report. Metadata availability supports a design discussion, not behavioural measurement or causal evidence. Missing count evidence limits a quality score, and missing impressions prevents a low-exposure inference.

## Assumptions and falsifiable evidence

The proposal assumes makers experience uncertainty that existing functionality does not resolve, required metadata is interpretable, and a summary reduces decision effort without misleading users or narrowing attention. Support would include observed difficulty assessing suitability, improved comprehension in prototype research, and a mature-cohort experiment with meaningful start-recording benefit and acceptable guardrails.

Evidence against it includes today's journey already solving the problem, labels being misunderstood, metadata gaps making summaries misleading, higher abandonment, unsuitable starts or concentration harms. A recording increase without corroboration of actual making would weaken the benefit interpretation.

Next: audit the current UI, observe makers across crafts and experience levels, interview designers about meaningful discovery, and validate metadata semantics. Use those results to choose between a summary, better explanation of existing filters, or no feature. See the product recommendation for the conditional experiment plan.
